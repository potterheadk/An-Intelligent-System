## How Artificial Neural Networks (AI) Mimic the Brain!
Modern Artificial Intelligence, like the large language models running today, is built on software designs called Artificial Neural Networks. They are deeply inspired by human biology, but they translate biological parts into math code.

* Neurons become "Nodes": In AI, a neuron is just a tiny mathematical function (a code placeholder that receives numbers, math-processes them, and passes them on).
* Synapses become "Weights": Instead of physical gaps and receptors, AI uses a number called a weight to represent connection strength.
* If a connection weight is high (e.g., 0.9), the signal passes through easily.
   * If a connection weight is low (e.g., 0.02), the signal is blocked.

## The "Learning" Comparison

* The Human Brain: Learns by modifying physical synapses via chemical signals.
* The AI Network: Learns using an algorithm called Backpropagation (a math calculation that checks if the AI made a mistake, then backtracks through the network to tweak the numerical "weights" until the system gets the right answer).

---
The Core Difference: While AI models use thousands of computers and
millions of watts of electricity to adjust their digital math weights,
your biological brain updates its physical structure in real-time on about
20 watts of power—roughly the energy needed to run a dim living room lightbulb!
